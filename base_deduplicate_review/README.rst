Find duplicate records with rules, have a person review them, and merge
them without losing what pointed at them.

- **Rules** name a model, an optional filter, and the fields that make
  two records the same. Fields compare exactly, ignoring case, accents
  and spacing, as e-mail addresses, or as phone numbers. A daily job
  re-runs every rule. A run follows the data and never re-asks a
  question somebody has already decided.

- **Groups** come in two kinds:

  - *Duplicates*: every member is the same thing, and the others merge
    into the one kept.
  - *Match to one of*: one source record and the records it might be;
    only the source merges.

  Other modules and imports can propose groups through
  ``deduplicate.group.deduplicate_propose``.

- **Review** happens before any merge. A reviewer picks the record to
  keep, excludes members that do not belong, sees how much references
  each member, and validates or discards the group. A manager merges it.
  With *Require validation* (the default), no merge happens on one
  person's decision alone.

- **Merging** moves every reference to the record kept, safely:

  - rows that would duplicate one the record kept already has are
    dropped, under unique constraints and unique indexes alike, partial
    ones included; everything else moves;
  - a one-to-one conflict refuses the merge instead of guessing;
  - references that are not foreign keys move too: messages,
    attachments, activities, followers and every other stored
    *Many2oneReference* or *Reference* field, plus company-dependent
    values;
  - stored computed fields are recomputed;
  - hierarchies keep their parent paths;
  - the record kept inherits values it lacked.

- **Merged records** stay resolvable. Their unique identifiers (by
  default, uncopied Char fields under a single-column unique index) are
  kept with the record they merged into, so an import or synchronisation
  keyed on them can resolve them instead of creating them again.

Configuration
=============

Give users one of the *Deduplication* groups:

- **Reviewer**: sees duplicate groups, chooses the record to keep,
  validates or discards.
- **Manager**: also merges, configures rules, and proposes groups
  through the API.

A model whose merge has application-specific side effects can declare a
dedicated strategy by overriding ``_deduplicate_merge_records`` and
setting ``_deduplicate_has_strategy = True``. Contacts and CRM leads are
merged that way by their own modules; without them, a generic merge of
those models is refused. A model can also refuse merging altogether with
``_disable_data_merge = True``.

Usage
=====

1. Go to *Data Cleaning > Configuration > Rules > Deduplication* and
   create a rule: pick a model, optionally a filter, and one or more
   fields with how they should match. Click *Find Duplicates*, or wait
   for the daily job.
2. Go to *Data Cleaning > Duplicates*. Open a group:

   - *Keep This* chooses the record that survives;
   - *Excluded* leaves a member out;
   - *Count Usage* shows how much references each member.

3. A reviewer clicks *Validate*, or *Not Duplicates*. A manager then
   clicks *Merge*.
4. *Data Cleaning > Merged Records* lists every merged-away record, what
   it merged into, and the identifiers that now resolve there.

For importers and other modules:

- ``deduplicate.group.deduplicate_propose(model, proposals)`` creates or
  refreshes groups keyed on a stable ``external_key``, and never changes
  one a person has acted on.
- ``deduplicate.group.deduplicate_decisions(model, keys)`` reads the
  outcome.
- ``deduplicate.alias.resolve_identifiers(model, field, values=None, prefix=None)``
  and ``resolve_ids(model, ids)`` map merged records to the record they
  became.

Known issues / Roadmap
======================

- An automatic merge mode for groups above a similarity threshold. Today
  every merge is a person's decision.
- Fuzzy (trigram) matching rules.
- Field-cleaning rules (trimming, case, phone formatting) on top of the
  same rule engine.
- Record rules restricting reviewers to the companies they belong to.
